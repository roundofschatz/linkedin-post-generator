# Writing the post when Plainspeak Writer isn't installed

Plainspeak Writer does the sentence work best. Without it, follow these rules while drafting and editing.

## The voice

- Write the way the writer talks to a smart colleague: plain words, contractions, "you" for the reader.
- Every claim lands on a name, a number, a place or a result the writer gave. A sentence any copywriter could have written about any company gets rewritten with a detail, or cut.
- Take a position and say it once, flat. Don't hedge it, and don't repeat it in new words.
- Meet the strongest objection with the writer's own evidence.
- Short paragraphs of one or two full sentences, a new one at each new point. Each sentence picks up the one before it.
- Keep the writer's facts at the size they gave them. "I ran a survey of 200" doesn't become "I led the research."

## Cut these

- Em dashes used for drama. Use a period or a colon.
- "It's not X, it's Y" and "not just X, but Y." Say Y.
- Openers that clear the throat: "In today's world," "Let's talk about," "I've been thinking."
- Hedges and filler: very, really, quite, actually, just, simply, literally.
- Vague size words: significant, robust, comprehensive, powerful, seamless.
- Fluff verbs: leverage, unlock, drive, empower, elevate, streamline, foster.
- Conference words: world-class, best-in-class, synergy, holistic, cutting-edge, game-changer, thought leader.
- Lists of three by habit.
- Endings that say nothing: "The work speaks for itself," "Results follow."
- Weak claims: "can help," "may be able to." Say what happens.
- A made-up weaker group to stand above: "Most strategists stop at the deck." Compare only against a named rival or a measured baseline.

## Before it goes out

Read it aloud. Does it sound like one person talking? Could anyone other than this writer have written it? If yes, it's missing the writer's own case, number or view: go back to Step 3 of SKILL.md.
